# Book One

We open with a request for the goddess to tell us of Achilleus' anger over the
pans put upon the Achains by the King Agememnon over the kidnapping of someone's
daughter. It is not immediately clear who. It seems Agememnon upset Apollo as he
dishonored Chryses, the priest of Apollo as they brought their ships to
Agememnon's host to ransom back the daughter. The Achains cried out in favor
that the priest is respected and the ransom be accepted.

Agamemnon tells the host to never be seen again and that he will never give the
girl back. The girl shall live in his house in Argos and go to his bed as a
companion. He tells them to leave or he will be angry. The old man obeys him and
leaves silently. 

The old man walks in solitude and prays many times over for the favor of Apollo.
He reminds Apollo of all the sacrifices he has made of burnt offerings and
building his temple. Apollo hears this and strode from Olympus in anger and let
loose an arrow that was terrible of a clash that rose from the bow of silver.

Apollo went after the mules and houndes, then the men themselves. For nine days
the host took on the god's arrows but Achilleus called to the assembly that they
should leave for home and to ask a prophet why Apollo is so angry at them. 

A man named Kalchas, son of Thestor, was a seer who knew of things that were and
things to come. He offered to speak to Achilleus but only if he promises to be
defended no matter what he says, since words can make men angry. Achilleus
promises that no harm will come to him.

The seer says that Apollo is angry that Agamemnon dishonored the priest whom he
dishonored by taking his daughter and refusing ransoms. Agamemnon tells the seer
that he is always saying evil things and that he wants Chryseis as he prizes her
above his own wife, Klytaimestra. Yet, he will give Chryseis back for the safety
of his people and that he requests a prize from the Archaians.

Achillius argues that there is no prize to give him where they are and that he
should return the girl with no prize now but the promise of prizes rewarded
thrice and four times over, if Zeus gives them the city of Troy to be plundered.

Agamemnon says that he will not be cheated of his prize and that Achilleus
should give him his own prize. If not, they shall take to the sea and be gone.
Achilleus argues that he has earned his prize and will not be taken away. More
importantly, the Arachaians followed him to win his honor and Melanos' from the
Trojans. He tells him that he fights more than his fair share but always
Agamemnon's reward is greater. 

This angers Agamemnon who says he is taking Chryseis and Achilleus girl too.
This angered Achilleus who considers drawing his sword. But, Athene descends
from the sky, sent by Hera, for she had love for both men. Athene appeared only
to Achilleus who asked her why she came and if it was due to the outrageous
behavior of Agamemnon. 

Athene answers that she came to stay his anger and that he needs to listen to
her and to not harm Agamemnon. Achilleus recognizes that he must obey her and
listen. He puts the sword back in its scabbard. Athene goes back to Olympus.
